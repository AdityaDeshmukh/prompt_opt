\section{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:setup-exp}

\paragraph{Task and data.}
Yelp sentiment transfer in the negative-to-positive direction. Content is
BERTScore~\citep{zhang2020bertscore} against the source (\texttt{roberta-large});
sentiment is a \texttt{bert-base-uncased} classifier fine-tuned on Yelp. Both
are on a $0$--$100$ scale.

\paragraph{Models.}
The policy is a \texttt{distilgpt2} backbone with a trained MLP adaptor head
(hidden width $2048$). It reads the source sentence and the scalar $\lmb$ and
emits a fixed-length $L=5$ token prompt. The head is initialized with
Xavier-uniform weights at gain $10^{-4}$, which drives its output logits to
zero, so \textbf{the initial prompt distribution is numerically uniform over
the full $50{,}257$-token vocabulary}: the measured policy entropy at step $0$
is $10.824904$ against $\ln 50257 = 10.824905$. This matters for GRPO and is
revisited in Section~\ref{sec:algos-caveat}. The task model is
\texttt{gpt2-xl}, \textbf{frozen} and inference-only in \texttt{bfloat16},
served through vLLM with top-$k=10$ sampling. Only the adaptor is trained.

\paragraph{Training.}
$12{,}000$ steps with a batch of $B=15$ sources and $\Ggrp=16$ prompts per
source. That is $240$ scored prompt--source rows per step, each scored with
$N=50$ task-model samples. The optimizer is Adam at learning rate $10^{-4}$
with gradient-norm clipping at $10$, and $\lmb \sim \mathcal{U}[0,1)$ is
redrawn per source per step. Training prompts are sampled from the policy with
top-$k=200$. \rrebel{} uses $\beta = 0.5$ and refreshes its reference every
$150$ steps. Both GRPO arms use $\beta_{\mathrm{KL}} = 0.04$ with a pinned
reference. The two $\beta$s are not comparable quantities: one is a regression
scale in Eq.~\eqref{eq:logratio}, the other a KL coefficient in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:grpo}.

\paragraph{Arms (main campaign, v4).}
Four arms, one training seed each, all trained to $12{,}000$ steps:
\rrebel{} $\ell_1$-std, \rrebel{} Huber-std, GRPO + entropy
($\alpha_H = 0.01$, uniform anchor), and GRPO with a base-LM reference
(Section~\ref{sec:algos-caveat}). Because v4 has a single training seed per arm,
training-seed variation comes from the earlier three-seed campaign (v3), which
used the same code, hyperparameters and step budget
(Section~\ref{sec:seed-replication}).

\paragraph{Evaluation protocol.}
All v4 numbers are on the \textbf{held-out Yelp test split ($500$ sentences)},
evaluated after training. At test time the policy decodes its prompt
\emph{greedily} for each $(x,\lmb)$. We sweep $\lmb$ over the $20$-point grid
$\{0, 0.05, \dots, 0.95\}$ (every point lies inside the training range), and
each prompt is scored by averaging $N = 50$ task-model samples. We report
\emph{expected} content $\mathbb{E}[c]$ and \emph{expected} sentiment
$\mathbb{E}[s]$ per $\lmb$, each averaged over task-model samples and test
sentences. Scorers run in \texttt{float32}.
The prompt is deterministic given $(x,\lmb)$, so the main randomness is the
task model's. We therefore repeat the final-checkpoint evaluation with
\textbf{five task-model seeds}, averaging over seeds first and then over
sentences. Evaluations ran on whichever eligible GPU was free. On a given GPU
model the greedy prompts are bit-identical across seeds. Across GPU models,
floating-point differences flip near-tied greedy choices for up to
\vHuberFlipMax\% (Huber-std) and \vGentFlipMax\% (GRPO + entropy) of the
(sentence, floor) prompts, and for up to \vGbaseFlipMax\% for GRPO with the
base-LM reference, whose prompt distribution is flatter. These flips are
between near-equivalent prompts: across the five seeds, each arm's mean reward
varies by at most \vSeedRangeMax{} points (standard deviation at most
\vSeedSDmax). The five-seed average therefore also averages over this
hardware variation. For comparison, re-running a $10$-sentence development
evaluation moved its score by $1.1$ points.
\textbf{Confidence intervals} are $95\%$ percentile bootstraps over test
sentences ($2{,}000$ resamples). The same resampled sentences are used for
every arm, so differences between arms are \emph{paired}. Resampling
seed-averaged per-sentence values captures both sentence-to-sentence variation
and residual Monte Carlo noise, because the latter is independent per sentence.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
Besides the frontier itself, we summarize each arm by:
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item \emph{mean reward}: the constrained reward of Eq.~\eqref{eq:reward}
        averaged over sentences and the $\lmb$ grid (the training objective,
        Eq.~\eqref{eq:objective});
  \item \emph{hypervolume}: the area dominated by the $20$ frontier points in the
        (content, sentiment) plane, with reference point $(0,0)$, as a percentage
        of the $100\times100$ box. This is the standard scalar summary of a
        tradeoff curve and does not depend on the reward's penalty
        constant;
  \item \emph{floors met}: the number of grid points at which
        $\mathbb{E}[c] \ge \tau$;
  \item $P(c \ge \tau)$: the per-sample probability that a rewrite meets its
        floor, averaged over the grid. This is the quantity the constrained
        reward actually penalizes;
  \item \emph{prompts per $\tau$}: the median, over the grid, of the number of
        distinct prompts the policy emits across the $500$ test sentences. This
        is a direct measure of input-dependence and of collapse.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Test-split learning curves.}
Each arm's checkpoints at steps $3000$, $6000$ and $9000$ are evaluated with the
same protocol (one task-model seed), giving matched test-split learning curves.

\paragraph{Supporting campaign (v3): training-seed replication on a small dev set.}
The earlier campaign trained five arms $\times$ three seeds with the same code
and budget. Its in-training evaluation ran on the \textbf{development split
capped at $10$ sentences}, a speed hack in the data loader, and its checkpoints
were later lost to a filesystem purge. Its scores survive only as the
experiment tracker's scalar history (Section~\ref{sec:provenance}). These are
$10$-sentence dev numbers, not test numbers. We use them only for what v4
cannot provide, namely the spread across \emph{training} seeds.

\paragraph{Compute.}
Each run occupies one GPU ($\ge\!40$\,GB, natively bf16) and runs as a chain of
$4$-hour scheduler cycles, each resuming from the most recent checkpoint. Step
time is $17$\,s on an H100 and $27$--$44$\,s on L40S/A100/RTX~A6000.
Generation by the frozen task model accounts for $97.6\%$ of step time; content
and sentiment scoring together take $2.3\%$, and the policy update under
$0.4\%$. A full $20$-point test evaluation takes $10$--$40$ minutes depending on
the GPU.
