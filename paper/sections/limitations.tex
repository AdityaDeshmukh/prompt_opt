\section{Limitations and threats to validity}
\label{sec:limitations}

We state these plainly, because several of them bound how strongly the headline
comparison can be read.

\paragraph{The evaluation metric is a 10-sentence dev score, not the test split.}
As Section~\ref{sec:setup-exp} states, the in-training evaluation runs on the
development split capped at $10$ sentences, and no run in this campaign was
ever evaluated on the $500$-sentence held-out test split. Every number in this
paper inherits that. The $6$-point family gap is large relative to the measured
$1$-point noise floor and we expect it to survive, but the confidence interval
at $n=10$ is much wider than ``three seeds'' suggests, the sub-$2$-point
variant gaps are not resolvable, and the absolute values are not comparable to
test-split numbers in the literature. This is the single most important caveat
on the results.

\paragraph{GRPO's reference policy is uniform, so its KL is not a trust region.}
The pinned $\polref$ is the policy at initialization, which in this
configuration is numerically the uniform distribution over the $50{,}257$-token
vocabulary (Section~\ref{sec:algos-caveat}). The KL penalty is therefore an
entropy bonus up to a constant, not a trust region toward a competent
reference, and the two GRPO arms are two settings of one anti-collapse lever
rather than two independent configurations. A GRPO baseline anchored on a
supervised-fine-tuned policy is the missing experiment, and until it is run our
claim should be read as being about GRPO \emph{in this single-update,
uniform-reference regime}, not about GRPO in general.

\paragraph{Optimizer state does not survive a resume.}
Checkpoints store the model's parameters only, and the optimizer is rebuilt on
each resume, so every run is really a sequence of $\sim\!20$ Adam warm restarts
with zeroed moments rather than one continuous trajectory. Cycle counts happen
to be close across arms ($63$--$65$), so this is not a systematic handicap to
either family, but it is a methodological defect and it should be fixed before
publication.

\paragraph{The tradeoff curve itself is not yet measured for these arms.}
As Sections~\ref{sec:tradeoff} and~\ref{sec:provenance} explain, the per-$\lmb$
frontier --- the figure this paper is nominally about --- is pending the
regeneration run. Everything reported here is $\lmb$-averaged. It remains possible that two arms
with similar average reward have differently-shaped curves, and the dominance
claim of Figure~\ref{fig:tradeoff} is about averaged operating points, not about
pointwise dominance across $\lmb$.

\paragraph{The rerun is single-seed.}
The regeneration campaign trains one seed per arm, not three. Its frontiers will
therefore be indicative rather than seed-averaged, and given GRPO's wide seed
spread ($26.3$--$34.7$ at step $11{,}500$) a single GRPO seed should not be read
as that arm's typical curve.

\paragraph{Evaluation noise and the number of seeds.}
Three seeds per arm and a $1.0$-point noise floor are enough to separate the
families ($6.0$ points) but not to rank variants within a family; we
consequently report the top two \rrebel{} variants as tied and draw no
conclusion between them. The noise floor is itself estimated from a single
repeated evaluation on $10$ sentences, so it is an order-of-magnitude figure
rather than a calibrated standard error.

\paragraph{The style classifier used at evaluation.}
The evaluation entry point requests the \emph{train}-split style classifier
rather than the test-split one. Since the same classifier scores every arm, this
cannot explain the family gap, but it does mean the absolute sentiment numbers
are not directly comparable to work that evaluates with the held-out
classifier.

\paragraph{Single task and single direction.}
All results are Yelp negative-to-positive with a \texttt{gpt2-xl} task model and
$5$-token prompts. We make no claim about other datasets, other transfer
directions, longer prompts, or larger task models. In particular, the inert-clip
argument of Section~\ref{sec:grpo} is a property of the single-update regime; a
pipeline that takes multiple epochs per rollout would keep the clip live --- a
real trust region, unlike the uniform-reference KL analysed in
Section~\ref{sec:algos-caveat} --- and might close some of the gap.
