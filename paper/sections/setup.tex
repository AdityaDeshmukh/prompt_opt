\section{Problem setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Prompted style transfer with a frozen task model.}
Let $x$ be a source sentence with an undesired attribute (here, a negative Yelp
review). A policy $\pol(\cdot \mid x, \lmb)$, conditioned on the source and
on a tradeoff parameter $\lmb$, emits a short discrete \emph{prompt}
$z = (z_1,\dots,z_L)$ of fixed length $L=5$ tokens. The prompt is
inserted into a fixed template together with the source,
\begin{equation}
  \texttt{template}(z, x) \;=\; \texttt{\{z\}\ "\{x\}"\ "} ,
\end{equation}
and a \emph{frozen}, inference-only task language model $\lm$ samples a
rewrite $y \sim \lm(\cdot \mid \texttt{template}(z,x))$. Only $\theta$ is
trained; $\lm$ receives no gradient. This is the prompt-optimization regime: the
action space is the discrete prompt, and the environment --- the task model plus
the scorers --- is a black box.

\paragraph{Two objectives.}
Each rewrite is scored on two axes, both on a $0$--$100$ scale:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
  \item \emph{content} $c(x,y) \in [0,100]$, a BERTScore-style similarity
        between the rewrite and its source, measuring how much of the original
        meaning survives;
  \item \emph{sentiment} $s(y) \in [0,100]$, the probability assigned to the
        target attribute by a fine-tuned BERT classifier.
\end{itemize}
The two are in genuine tension: copying $x$ verbatim maximizes $c$ and leaves
$s$ at the source value, while emitting a generic positive sentence maximizes
$s$ and destroys $c$.

\paragraph{The tradeoff curve.}
$\lmb \in [0,1]$ indexes a content floor $\tau = 100\lmb$. For each floor, the
curve has one point: the expected content and expected sentiment that the
policy delivers when told that floor,
\begin{equation}
  \bigl(\,\mathbb{E}[c \mid \tau],\; \mathbb{E}[s \mid \tau]\,\bigr),
  \qquad
  \mathbb{E}[\,\cdot \mid \tau] = \mathbb{E}_{x}\,
  \mathbb{E}_{z\sim\pol(\cdot\mid x,\lmb)}\,
  \mathbb{E}_{y\sim\lm(\cdot\mid \texttt{template}(z,x))}[\,\cdot\,].
  \label{eq:curve}
\end{equation}
Sweeping $\lmb$ from $0$ (no content requirement --- pure sentiment) to $1$
(near-verbatim content required) traces the curve. The natural problem this
curve depicts is the $\varepsilon$-constraint program: for every floor, the
most sentiment that can be had with the floor met in expectation,
\begin{equation}
  \max_{\pi(\cdot\mid x,\lmb)}\; \mathbb{E}[s \mid x, \tau]
  \quad\text{s.t.}\quad \mathbb{E}[c \mid x, \tau] \ge \tau
  \qquad\text{for every } (x,\lmb).
  \label{eq:epsconstraint}
\end{equation}
We study two ways of turning this into a reward. Write $\bar c(x,z)$ and
$\bar s(x,z)$ for a prompt's expected content and sentiment over the task
model's samples.

\paragraph{Reward I: a floor on every sample.}
The reward used in our earlier campaigns puts the floor on each individual
rewrite,
\begin{equation}
  \Rlam(z,x) \;=\;
  \begin{cases}
    s(y), & c(x,y) \;\ge\; 100\lmb, \\[2pt]
    0.01\,\bigl(c(x,y) - 100\lmb\bigr), & \text{otherwise,}
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:reward}
\end{equation}
so that reward equals the sentiment score wherever the floor is met and
degrades to a small penalty proportional to the shortfall otherwise. Because
$\lm$ is stochastic, the reward used for learning is a Monte Carlo average over
$N = 50$ task-model samples per (prompt, source) pair,
\begin{equation}
  \hat{r}_{\lmb}(z,x) \;=\; \frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N} \Rlam(z, x; y_n),
  \qquad y_n \sim \lm(\cdot \mid \texttt{template}(z,x)).
  \label{eq:mcreward}
\end{equation}
Its expectation is $\mathbb{E}[s\,\mathbf{1}\{c\ge\tau\}] -
0.01\,\mathbb{E}[(\tau-c)_+]$: sentiment weighted by the \emph{probability}
that a sample meets the floor. This is a surrogate for a chance constraint, not
for Eq.~\eqref{eq:epsconstraint}. Nothing in it ties $\mathbb{E}[c\mid\tau]$ to
$\tau$, and Section~\ref{sec:th-constraint} shows that, combined with a policy
that settles on a few prompts, it is what makes the floors of the resulting
curve cluster and miss the line $\mathbb{E}[c]=\tau$.

\paragraph{Reward II: a floor on the expected content.}
The reward behind our main results imposes Eq.~\eqref{eq:epsconstraint}
through a quadratic penalty on the shortfall of the \emph{policy's} expected
content,
\begin{equation}
  \max_{\pi(\cdot\mid x,\lmb)}\;
  \mathbb{E}_{z\sim\pi}[\bar s(x,z)]
  \;-\; \frac{\nu}{2}\,\Bigl(\tau - \mathbb{E}_{z\sim\pi}[\bar c(x,z)]\Bigr)_+^{2}.
  \label{eq:penalty}
\end{equation}
Its gradient with respect to $\pi$ is that of a \emph{linear} per-rollout
reward, $\bar s + \mu\,\bar c$, with multiplier
$\mu = \nu\,(\tau - \mathbb{E}_\pi[\bar c])_+$. Every training group already
contains $\Ggrp$ prompts drawn from $\pi(\cdot\mid x,\lmb)$, so the group's
mean content is an unbiased estimate of $\mathbb{E}_\pi[\bar c \mid x,\lmb]$.
Prompt $z_i$ of a group therefore receives
\begin{equation}
  r_i \;=\; \hat s_i + \mu_g\,(\hat c_i - \tau),
  \qquad
  \mu_g \;=\; \nu\,\Bigl(\tau - \tfrac{1}{\Ggrp}\textstyle\sum_{j=1}^{\Ggrp} \hat c_j\Bigr)_{\!+},
  \label{eq:reward-exp}
\end{equation}
where $\hat s_i, \hat c_i$ are the $N$-sample means. When the group already
meets the floor on average, $\mu_g = 0$ and the reward is plain sentiment. When
it falls short, content is weighted in proportion to the shortfall. We use
$\nu = 5$. The implementation divides $r_i$ by $1+\mu_g$ to keep its range
bounded; under group standardization this rescaling, and the shift by
$-\mu_g\tau$, have no effect (Lemma~\ref{lem:invariance}). Reward~II is linear in
$\hat s$ and $\hat c$, so its Monte Carlo estimate is unbiased, unlike the
indicator in Eq.~\eqref{eq:reward}. Section~\ref{sec:th-constraint} derives what
it converges to, and why the multiplier is set this way rather than by an exact
projection or by dual ascent.

\paragraph{Objective: one policy for the whole curve.}
During training $\lmb$ is drawn afresh per source, $\lmb \sim \mathcal{U}[0,1]$,
and supplied to the policy as a conditioning input. A single set of parameters
$\theta$ must therefore serve every point on the curve, so that at test time the
frontier is traced by varying the conditioning input rather than by retraining:
\begin{equation}
  \max_{\theta}\;
  \mathbb{E}_{\lmb \sim \mathcal{U}[0,1]}\;
  \mathbb{E}_{x}\;
  \mathbb{E}_{z \sim \pol(\cdot \mid x, \lmb)}\bigl[\hat r_{\lmb}(z,x)\bigr]
  \label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
for Reward~I, and the $\lmb$- and $x$-average of Eq.~\eqref{eq:penalty} for
Reward~II, whose gradient the per-rollout reward of Eq.~\eqref{eq:reward-exp}
supplies. This conditioning is what makes the problem
multi-objective rather than a set of independent single-objective runs, and it
is the reason entropy collapse is especially damaging here: a policy that
collapses onto one prompt becomes $\lmb$-independent and degenerates to a single
point instead of a curve.
