\begin{abstract}
We study prompt optimization for a multi-objective text task, where a single
policy must trace a whole tradeoff curve rather than reach one operating point.
A $\lmb$-conditioned policy emits a short discrete prompt that steers a
\emph{frozen} task language model. $\lmb$ sets a floor on content
preservation, and sweeping it traces a content-versus-sentiment curve. We
train the policy with \rrebel{}, which regresses the $\beta$-scaled log-ratio
difference of two sampled prompts onto their reward difference under a robust
discrepancy, with rewards standardized within each group of samples. We
develop its theory in the style of DPO. For any discrepancy and any
full-support sampler, the population minimizer is a KL-regularized optimum.
Group standardization replaces one global temperature with a per-context one,
giving every context the same trust-region radius. Pairwise differencing
symmetrizes reward noise, so robust discrepancies cost no bias. And
single-update GRPO without a KL term is exactly \rrebel{}-$\ell_2$-std with
its reference refreshed at every step. In this problem, the within-group reward
scale varies by a factor of \sRangeQ{} across contexts, which is the regime
where standardization matters. On the $\vNsent$-sentence Yelp test split,
\rrebel{} with a Huber discrepancy has the best tradeoff curve: hypervolume
$\vHuberHV$ against $\vGentHV$ for the strongest GRPO baseline, and mean
constrained reward $\vHuberRew$ against $\vGentRew$, with paired $95\%$
intervals that exclude zero. When a policy may mix its prompts, the Huber curve
lies above the GRPO curve at every content level. A GRPO baseline given a
genuine trust region toward the pretrained backbone is the weakest arm. The
$\ell_1$ variant reaches a comparable frontier but, late in training, assigns
operating points to floors poorly; early stopping on the development set
recovers it. Finally, although the policies read the input sentence, each one
has learned a small $\lmb$-indexed library of interpretable prompts.
\end{abstract}
